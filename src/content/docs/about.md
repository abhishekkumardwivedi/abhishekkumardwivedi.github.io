## From hardware to platform software

I began close to the hardware, developing firmware and boot software for ARM-based systems. That foundation included board bring-up, bootloaders, low-level debugging, memory and peripheral behaviour, and the practical use of hardware debuggers and trace tools. It taught me to investigate a failure across electrical, timing, firmware, and software boundaries instead of assuming that every visible software symptom is caused by software.

I remain comfortable reading schematics, understanding PCB-level connectivity, reviewing hardware interfaces, and working with mechanical and electrical teams. My interests also extend to 3D CAD interpretation, simulation, rapid prototyping, and 3D printing—useful when software is only one part of a physical product.

## Linux, Android, and connected platforms

As my work expanded upward through the stack, I worked across Embedded Linux, Android/AOSP, middleware, native services, HALs, frameworks, kernels, boot flows, and BSP integration. My experience includes telephony and RIL, audio, camera and media pipelines, Wi‑Fi and Bluetooth integration, USB and serial interfaces, and communication buses such as SPI, I²C, and CAN.

I have worked with Yocto-based platforms, virtualization and hypervisors, QEMU, U-Boot and GRUB, system services, device management, and cross-layer performance and stability problems. This breadth allows me to reason about a product as a complete execution environment—from application behaviour to services, operating-system policy, drivers, shared memory, peripherals, and silicon capabilities.

Over different phases of my career, I contributed to mobile and consumer products, connected devices, set-top boxes, IoT middleware, and modular computing platforms. I have participated in work involving Samsung platforms, Intel initiatives, Linaro and Google’s Project Ara ecosystem, and large-scale consumer and automotive programmes. These environments taught me how architecture changes when a prototype must become a maintainable, secure, certifiable, and production-ready product.

## Security, identity, and trusted platforms

Security has been a recurring part of my engineering work rather than a separate feature added at the end. I have worked with secure boot chains, trusted execution environments, hardware-backed identity, key provisioning, Android Keystore, SELinux policy, platform hardening, sandboxing, cryptographic integration, and secure update concepts.

My projects have included device certificates and CSR flows backed by secure storage, native services and HAL interfaces, boot and firmware trust, virtualization boundaries, and security-lifecycle thinking influenced by automotive cybersecurity practices. I approach security as a system property involving hardware roots of trust, software architecture, manufacturing, provisioning, operations, updates, diagnostics, and eventual decommissioning.

## IoT, sensing, vision, and intelligent edge systems

I have consistently explored technologies beyond my formal assignments. My hands-on work has included wireless sensors, Bluetooth Low Energy, mesh and gateway concepts, MQTT and connected telemetry, Android-to-device communication, cloud-connected embedded prototypes, and real-world sensor integration.

Computer vision and machine learning became another important learning path. I experimented with object detection and tracking, camera-based systems, GPU-assisted processing, neural-network frameworks, and the practical limitations of earlier generations of vision models and hardware. Today, that experience connects naturally with edge inference, heterogeneous CPU/GPU/NPU execution, perception pipelines, model export and compilation, and the deployment constraints of embedded and automotive systems.

I also continue to practise algorithms and data structures because architectural responsibility should not replace computational thinking. Understanding complexity, memory behaviour, scheduling, concurrency, and data movement remains valuable when assessing system performance or accelerator deployment.

## Automotive and software-defined vehicles

My current professional focus is automotive system and product architecture, especially software-defined vehicles and digital cockpits. The work brings together Android Automotive, Embedded Linux, QNX-hosted systems, Qualcomm automotive compute, vehicle interfaces, displays and cameras, voice and connectivity, diagnostics, OTA, security, safety considerations, and supplier integration.

In this environment, architecture is not only a block diagram. It must account for startup sequencing, resource partitioning, mixed criticality, IPC, data ownership, timing, failure management, update compatibility, diagnostics, cybersecurity, and the realities of distributed development across OEMs, Tier-1 suppliers, semiconductor vendors, and software teams.

My role has evolved from solving individual technical problems to defining system concepts, decomposing requirements, shaping component boundaries, reviewing designs, identifying technical gaps, coordinating suppliers, guiding teams, managing risks, and maintaining alignment between product intent and engineering delivery.

## Autonomy, simulation, and systems engineering

I am extending this experience toward autonomous and intelligent vehicle systems. My work includes conceptual perception architectures, multimodal sensor pipelines, bird’s-eye-view representations, fusion, prediction, planning, control, safety supervision, and autonomous-vehicle software-in-the-loop environments.

I am particularly interested in how models move from research frameworks into real products: model selection and training, quantisation, graph export, compilation, backend partitioning, heterogeneous acceleration, memory movement, scheduling, observability, and safety-aware integration with the wider vehicle stack.

Alongside this, I am building an AI-native systems-engineering platform for modelling automotive hardware, software, communication, deployment, relationships, and traceability. It reflects a recurring concern in my career: complex systems become difficult not because teams lack individual expertise, but because their shared architectural picture becomes fragmented.

## How I approach engineering

- **Begin with the product and its operating environment.** Technology choices should follow user value, constraints, safety, security, lifecycle, and business intent.
- **Think across layers.** A robust decision considers hardware, operating systems, middleware, applications, networks, data, backend services, manufacturing, and operations.
- **Make interfaces and failure behaviour explicit.** Ownership, timing, data contracts, resource limits, diagnostics, fallbacks, and traceability are central architectural concerns.
- **Remain close to implementation.** Code, logs, traces, schematics, measurements, and prototypes keep architecture honest.
- **Design for evolution.** A platform should accommodate new hardware, features, regulations, product variants, and deployment models without repeated reinvention.
- **Build a common system view.** Complex products succeed when hardware, software, safety, security, product, and supplier teams can reason from the same model.

## What brings it together

The common thread across my work is curiosity about complete systems. I enjoy moving between strategy and implementation: understanding a market or customer problem, shaping the product concept, designing the architecture, investigating a low-level failure, and helping teams turn the result into a sustainable platform.

I am most energised by opportunities that combine complex-system architecture, embedded and platform engineering, intelligent edge computing, product ownership, and technical leadership—particularly in automotive and other safety-relevant cyber-physical products.

Outside formal work, I enjoy learning, writing, prototyping, fitness, travel, and exploring how technology can make physical systems safer, more understandable, and more useful.
